A

Join the Apple Developer Program

US $99 / year · approval usually within 48 hours
TrapDo not pick Organization / Company unless you have a registered legal business — it requires a D-U-N-S number and can take weeks. Individual accounts can sell paid apps just the same.
B

Agreements, tax & banking

The phase first-timers discover too late — skip only if your app is 100% free
The big oneThe Paid Apps agreement must read Active — not Pending — before you can submit an app that charges money, and bank verification alone can take days. Start this phase early, in parallel with everything else, so it isn't the thing you're stuck waiting on at the end.
C

Create your app record

Reserve the name and register your app's identity
TrapThe app name must be unique across the entire App Store and at most 30 characters. Have a backup ready — a distinct display name like "YourApp: Short Tagline" — and keep the short version as the subtitle.
D

In-app purchases & subscriptions

Skip this phase entirely if your app is free with no purchases
TrapBrand-new purchases are reviewed together with your first app version — you attach them to the 1.0 submission in Phase G, not before. Until then they sit as "Ready to Submit", which is normal, not an error.
E

Store listing, privacy & assets

Everything reviewers and customers see — most first rejections happen here
TrapReviewers open your links. The privacy policy URL must actually load, and your privacy answers must match what the app really does — a mismatch between the label, the policy, and the binary is one of the most common first-time rejections.
F

Upload the build

From Xcode to App Store Connect
G

Submit for review

Attach everything, answer honestly, send it
If it gets rejectedDon't panic — first submissions get rejected all the time, usually for small fixable things. Read the message in the Resolution Center, fix exactly what it names, and resubmit; the second review is usually faster. It's a conversation, not a verdict.

Approved? You're live 🎉

Three small habits for your first week on the store.

  • →
    Release it — if you chose manual release, the button is on your app's page in App Store Connect.
  • →
    Watch crashes — Xcode's Organizer and App Store Connect show crash reports from real devices. Quiet is good news.
  • →
    Reply to early reviews — responses come from you, the developer, and first users notice. Updates go through review too, but usually faster.
